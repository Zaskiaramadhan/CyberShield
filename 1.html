    Social Engineering Explainer

    Attackers trick people instead of hacking machines. Press Play on each card to watch how the scam unfolds.

    Email

    Phishing

    A fake email pretends to be a real company to steal your login details.

    1. 1An email says "Your account will be locked in 24 hours!"
    2. 2You click the link inside.
    3. 3A fake login page, copied from the real one, opens.
    4. 4You type your password and the attacker receives it.

    Red flags: urgent threats, a sender address that does not match the company, and a link that goes to another domain.

    Free stuff

    Baiting

    Something tempting is left for you to find, such as a free download or a USB drive.

    1. 1You find a USB drive labelled "Salary 2026" or see a "free premium app" ad.
    2. 2Curiosity wins and you plug it in or install it.
    3. 3Malware installs silently in the background.
    4. 4The attacker gets access to your files and accounts.

    Red flags: "too good to be true" offers and unknown devices. Never plug in a USB you did not buy, and install apps only from official stores.

    SMS

    Smishing

    Phishing sent by text message or chat, often with a short link.

    Your parcel is on hold. Pay the Rp5.000 fee at bit.ly/cek-paket
    1. 1An urgent SMS arrives from an unknown number.
    2. 2You tap the shortened link.
    3. 3A fake payment page or an .apk download opens.
    4. 4Your card details are stolen, or the app takes over your phone.

    Red flags: unknown sender, a shortened link, and a request for a small payment. Never install an .apk sent by SMS or chat.

    Personal data

    Identity Theft

    A criminal uses your personal information pretending to be you.

    1. 1The attacker collects your name, birth date and phone number from public posts or leaked data.
    2. 2They use it to answer security questions or to trick customer support.
    3. 3They take over your accounts or apply for loans in your name.
    4. 4You are left with debt, lost accounts and a long recovery.

    Protect yourself: share less publicly, turn on two-factor authentication, and check your accounts for activity you do not recognise.
